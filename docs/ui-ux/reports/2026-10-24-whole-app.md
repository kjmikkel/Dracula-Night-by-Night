# UI/UX pass: Dracula Night by Night, whole app, 24 October 2026

## 1. Scope

- **Scope:** whole app (owner confirmed at the gate).
- **Audience:** `docs/audiences.md`, confirmed by the owner. The rules this pass leans on:
  - "defaults protect the purist; settings serve the casual reader" (`docs/audiences.md:27`);
  - a first-time reader is put off by any spoiler, including just learning who exists (`docs/audiences.md:40`);
  - "the stricter one sets the default" (`docs/audiences.md:47`);
  - the access needs table, including **Reading fatigue / dyslexia** (`docs/audiences.md:75`).
- **Platform:** Android (Jetpack Compose).
- **Observed:** run on the `Dracula_Root_API_37` emulator (Android 17, 1080 × 2400, 420 dpi), debug build of 1.0.0 from
  `feat/204-whos-who` at 145703a. App data was cleared at the start, with the owner's approval.
- **Inventory changes since last run:** first run of this pass. The 16 October accessibility spike
  (`docs/accessibility-spike.md`) is the prior art; all eleven of its issues (#113–#123) are now closed.
- **Web access:** yes.
- **Fan-out:** none. The inventory has 4+ areas with their own code, which would normally mean one reviewer per area.
  Here the findings came out of one serial walk on one device, so the main agent reviewed every area itself.

## 2. Summary

The app is in good shape. Accessibility basics hold up on a real device as well as in the ratchet: labels, headings,
focus rings, target sizes and dark mode. The two medium problems are a spoiler and line length. The filter sheet gives a first-time reader the whole cast on
day one. In landscape, the reader's body text runs to about 140 characters a line. The rest is low-impact polish in
the reader and the Mode dialog.

**Top problems** (the two medium findings, then the low one every reader meets)
- **F-1:** the filter sheet names Van Helsing, Lucy, Seward and the Demeter before the reader has met them, which
  breaks the spoiler rule Who's who was built around.
- **F-4:** in phone landscape the reader's body text spans the whole screen width, about 140 characters a line.
- **F-2:** the reader's app bar cuts the author's name short at default text size ("Jonathan Har…", "Arthur Holm…").
  It ranks above F-3 and F-5 because it shows at default settings.

**Keep**
- The **keyboard focus ring** is clearly visible (double ring on the Read button, `shots/k1-timeline-tab4.png`). #113
  is fixed on the device, not only in `FocusRingTest`.
- **Empty states say what to do next.** Bookmarks: "No bookmarks yet. Tap ☆ Bookmark while reading to keep an entry
  here." Who's who: "People appear here as you meet them in the story." (`shots/s7-bookmarks-empty.png`,
  `shots/s4-whoswho-first.png`).
- **The Mode dialog says what switching does** before Choose is pressed ("Switching mode keeps what you have read, but
  entries may lock or unlock.", `shots/d1-mode-free.png`).
- **The purist defaults are visible and switchable.** The telegram keeps both Stoker's heading and "From … to …" by
  default, with the setting one tap away (#177, `shots/s3-reader-telegram.png`).
- **The accessibility ratchet** ran on this commit and passed: 194 cases. Its baseline holds 6 accepted violations,
  all #139: the filter sheet's scrim has no role, because Material 3 builds it privately.

## 4. Standards

No `docs/ui-ux/standards.md` exists yet, so every standard below is `researched`. The verdicts in §5 are measured
against these sources. The owner decides at hand-over which of them become project standards.

### A11Y-1: Text resizes to 200 % without loss of content
Rule: At 200 % text, no text or control is clipped, truncated or hidden, unless its full content is shown on focus
and something shows that more exists.
Source: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/resize-text.html
Status: researched 2026-10-24

### NAV-1: Top app bar headlines aren't truncated
Rule: An app bar's headline is not truncated. A longer headline uses a medium or large flexible app bar, where it
can wrap to a second line.
Source: https://m3.material.io/components/app-bars/guidelines ("Don't truncate the headline text.")
Status: researched 2026-10-24

### TYPE-1: Body text is no more than 80 characters wide
Rule: A block of body text is at most 80 characters or glyphs wide.
Source: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/visual-presentation.html (SC 1.4.8, AAA). The project's
own audience names reading fatigue and dyslexia as a need (`docs/audiences.md:75`), which is who this criterion
protects.
Status: researched 2026-10-24

### CONTENT-1: Nothing reveals a person before the entry that introduces them
Rule: Nothing in the app shows that a person or voice exists before the entry that introduces them: no name, group
or hint. A voice such as the Demeter's log counts.
Source: `docs/audiences.md:40`; `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-18-whos-who-design.md:153-154`
Status: researched 2026-10-24

### CONTENT-2: The same choice is offered the same way everywhere
Rule: The same options appear in the same order, with the same words, wherever the app offers them, so the reader
recognises them instead of recalling what they meant.
Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/ (#4 Consistency and standards, internal
consistency; #6 Recognition rather than recall)
Status: researched 2026-10-24

## 5. Findings

### F-1 · S5 Filter sheet · Fails · CONTENT-1
Evidence: `shots/s5-filter-sheet.png`: on the first night, with only Bram Stoker's memorandum arrived, the Voices
chips are Jonathan, Mina, Seward, Lucy, Van Helsing, Demeter, Others. Code: `timeline/FilterSheet.kt:54` lists
`Voice.entries`, the fixed list in `domain/Voice.kt`.
Observed: run
What's wrong: every chip but Others names someone the reader hasn't met yet. Four of them (Seward, Lucy, Van
Helsing, Demeter) aren't introduced for weeks, so the sheet tells a first-time reader on day one that they matter to
the story. That is the "who exists" spoiler that Who's who was designed to avoid.
Impact: medium · Effort: M

### F-2 · S3 Reader · Fails · NAV-1
Evidence: `shots/s3-reader-diary.png` ("Jonathan Har…"), `shots/s3-reader-telegram.png` ("Arthur Holm…"), at default
text size on a 411 dp wide phone. Code: `reader/ReaderScreen.kt:172` (`maxLines = 1, overflow = TextOverflow.Ellipsis`).
Observed: run
What's wrong: the app bar holds Back, the author's name, Who's who and ☆ Bookmark as text. At normal text size on
an ordinary phone, a 15-character name like "Jonathan Harker" no longer fits and is cut off. The spike on 16 October,
before Who's who (#204) was in the bar, saw the name hidden only at 200 % (#118).
Impact: low (the document header below usually names the writer again) · Effort: S
Also: P-1 is the same crowded bar at 200 %.

### F-3 · S3 Reader · Partly meets · A11Y-1
Evidence: `shots/s3-reader-telegram-200.png`: the follow switch reads "✓ Telegra…". Code: `reader/ReaderScreen.kt:244`
(`widthIn(max = 120.dp)`, ellipsis).
Observed: run
What's wrong: at 200 % the reader's body text and document header wrap. The follow switch's filter label is cut to
120 dp instead, with no way to see it in full. The accessibility tree carries the whole word ("Telegrams" in the
uiautomator dump), but a sighted reader only sees "Telegra…".
Impact: low · Effort: S

### F-4 · S3 Reader · Fails · TYPE-1
Evidence: `shots/s8-landscape.png`: in phone landscape the body text node spans x = 53–2347 px, about 870 dp, roughly
140 characters a line at Normal size. Code: `reader/ReaderScreen.kt:287` caps only newspapers (`NEWSPAPER_COLUMN = 520.dp`).
Observed: run
What's wrong: every document type except newspapers runs the full width of a landscape phone. That is well past 80
characters a line, which is hard to follow for the reading-fatigue and dyslexic readers `docs/audiences.md` names.
Impact: medium · Effort: S
Also: P-3 (the bars' height on the same screen).

### F-5 · D1 How to read · Fails · CONTENT-2
Evidence: `shots/s1-welcome.png` vs `shots/d1-mode-dialog.png`. Welcome lists In the same tempo, On the actual days,
Read freely, each with a line on what it means. The dialog lists On the actual days first and gives names only. Code:
`timeline/ModeDialog.kt:32-36`. Its KDoc still says "Minimal mode picker; plan 2 replaces it".
Observed: run
What's wrong: a reader changing mode months after Welcome sees the options in another order, without the line that
said what each one does.
Impact: low · Effort: S

## 6. Suggested issues

### Filter sheet: show only the voices the reader has met (#220)
Problem: on the first night the filter sheet's Voices chips name Van Helsing, Lucy, Seward and the Demeter (S5).
Evidence: F-1
Standard: CONTENT-1 (`docs/audiences.md:40`, whos-who spec :153)
Suggested fix: show a voice chip only once an entry in that voice has arrived, in `Voice` order. Others stays.
This is the same rule `WhosWhoView` applies to people. Clear All still empties a saved filter, so no unmet chip has
to stay visible.
Impact / effort: medium / M

### Reader: cap body text at a readable width (#221)
Problem: in phone landscape the reader's text runs about 140 characters a line (S3).
Evidence: F-4
Standard: TYPE-1 (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/visual-presentation.html)
Suggested fix: give `Body` a `widthIn(max = …)` for every document type, centred, as `NEWSPAPER_COLUMN` already does
for newspapers. The measurement above (about 870 dp for 140 characters) gives about 6.2 dp a character, so 80
characters is about 500 dp. Reusing the newspaper's 520 dp would keep one column width for every document. Add a landscape-phone window to the ratchet's
reader cases so this is checked.
Impact / effort: medium / S

### Reader: stop truncating the author in the app bar (#222)
Problem: "Jonathan Har…", "Arthur Holm…" at normal text size on a standard phone (S3).
Evidence: F-2
Standard: NAV-1 (https://m3.material.io/components/app-bars/guidelines)
Suggested fix: in `ReaderScreen.kt:172`, let the author's name wrap to two lines (`maxLines = 2`), which is what
Material's medium flexible app bar does. Applying NAV-1 to the other screens' app bars is for the second pass.
Impact / effort: low / S

### Reader: show the follow switch's whole filter label at large text (#223)
Problem: at 200 % the follow switch reads "Telegra…" (S3).
Evidence: F-3
Standard: A11Y-1 (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/resize-text.html)
Suggested fix: at large text only, drop the 120 dp cap so the segmented row can use the space between the arrows.
At large text, Previous and Next are already shrunk to ‹ and ›.
Impact / effort: low / S

### Mode dialog: match Welcome's order and explanations (#224)
Problem: Settings → Mode lists the modes in a different order from Welcome, without their explanations (D1).
Evidence: F-5
Standard: CONTENT-2 (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)
Suggested fix: reorder `ModeDialog`'s options to tempo, actual days, freely. Add Welcome's one-line description under
each, sharing the strings with `WelcomeScreen`. Update the stale KDoc.
Impact / effort: low / S

## 8. Zoo and second pass

`a11y/Cases.kt`, which renders every screen and dialog in six looks but shows nothing to a person. Suggestion: a
debug-only gallery screen in the project's own idiom, built on the same `Case` seeds, so a look-and-feel decision is
made once while looking at it.
- **Components and states:**
  - top app bar: short and long author, 100 % and 200 %;
  - document header: all seven types;
  - body text: phone portrait, phone landscape, tablet;
  - filter chip: selected and unselected, met and not yet met;
  - segmented button: short and long label at 200 %;
  - expandable list row: open and closed;
  - Tonight card: full and compact;
  - alert dialog: D1–D7;
  - empty state: S4, S7.
- **Decide while looking at it:** the column width for TYPE-1, and P-1 and P-3.

## 9. Ratchets

**Added on 24 October, after hand-over.** All three run in the accessibility ratchet, and the full unit suite passes
(631 tests). The baseline grew from 6 to 77 known violations:

| rule | standard | issue | baseline lines | where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `heading-truncated` | NAV-1 | #222 | 24 | reader: diary, telegram, phonograph, log, newspaper; two panes, portrait tablet (normal looks) |
| `line-length` | TYPE-1 | #221 | 17 | new landscape-phone reader cases: diary, letter, memo, phonograph, log; every two-pane tablet case |
| `spoiler` | CONTENT-1 | #220 | 30 | filter sheet: Mina, Seward, Lucy, Van Helsing, Demeter in all six looks |

- Code: `a11y/Rules.kt`, `a11y/Cases.kt` (`reader-*-landscape` cases, `Expectations.unmet`). Unit tests are in
  `a11y/ReadingRulesTest.kt`. `a11y/SpoilerCasesTest.kt` keeps the unmet-voice list in step with the story.
- The ratchet found more than the estimates below. Text runs over 80 characters in both tablet two-pane landscape
  cases and both timeline-hidden cases. The author's name is cut off in two-pane portrait. The newspaper's name is
  cut off too.
- Who's who's empty state now checks that no names show. It passes.

The suggestions as written at hand-over:

- **Headlines never ellipsize (NAV-1).** The ratchet's "clipped" rule reports an ellipsis only when what's left
  is very short, under three times the font size (`a11y/Rules.kt:126-135`). "Jonathan Har…" is longer than that, so
  F-2 doesn't show up in the ratchet today.
  - Measures: a node with `heading()` whose layout would ellipsize, at any width.
  - Baseline today: an estimate, not measured. Diary and telegram were seen truncated. Phonograph ("Dr. John
    Seward") and log ("Captain of the Demeter") have longer names, so likely 4 of the 7 reader cases.
  - Runs in: `app-tests`, through `RatchetTest`.
  - Locks in: F-2.
- **Reading width (TYPE-1).** Add a landscape-phone window (about 915 × 411 dp) to the reader cases, and a rule that
  the body text node is at most 80 average character widths wide.
  - Baseline today: an estimate of 7 cases (every document type but newspapers).
  - Runs in: `app-tests`.
  - Locks in: F-4.
- **No spoilers outside the text (CONTENT-1).** Easy to reintroduce: any new list of people or voices can leak the
  cast.
  - Measures: a unit test beside `domain/WhosWhoViewTest.kt`. For every arrival point in the story, every name shown
    in the filter sheet and Who's who belongs to an entry already arrived.
  - Baseline today: F-1's chips, 6 unmet voices on day one.
  - Runs in: `app-tests`.
  - Locks in: F-1.
